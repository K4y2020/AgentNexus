# Cine Creative 01: live result

## Evidence

On 2026-09-08, Cine v19 was asked to read the current EP1 S02 script and
storyboard and return a text-only six-segment revision, without seeing the
editor's B draft. Request item: `b470a903a0eb4c1b81d8e17ed2a6d4e4` in Topic
`a5a6835bd2e1e298fbf03c05e3d755c6`. The session returned to idle with a full
response identifying itself as CINE-CREATIVE-01. This is a single live trial,
not a controlled before/after model comparison or independent blind review.

## Verdict: revision not accepted

The model correctly identified missing spatial anchors, the seven-second
rescue problem, and the ending's excessive explanatory broadcast. It retained
the comic genre and produced six segments totaling 62 seconds. However:

- Its proposed E01-10B already warns the man to put down the cup; E01-11B
  adds another long warning before he finishes drinking. The diagnosed
  delay problem remains. Facing away does not establish inability to hear.
- A public free trial sign motivates taking a drink, but does not explain
  why a dangerous memory-erasing concentrate is available to an unprocessed
  arrival. Calling it destructive testing adds another unsupported rule.
- It replaces a seven-second leap with a 5.5-second sliding rescue, not a
  simpler causally timed action. It adds complicated knee movement, wet-floor
  reflections and extreme neck movement without narrative necessity.
- The ending adds smoke, empty-circle eyes, flashing lamps, five metres of
  printed paper, wardrobe removal, hiding and distress in 10.5 seconds.
  This increases production load and risks changing the approved visual style.
- The cup is alternately plastic and paper. New props are disclosed, but
  disclosure alone does not resolve continuity or justify additions.

## Next revision constraints

Ask Cine to revise only the failed parts, not invent a larger spectacle.
Require event order to demonstrate why the warning cannot prevent the swallow.
Preserve the paper cup and approved character design. Use one practical comic
consequence rather than simultaneous effects. Each claimed fix must cite the
new moment that resolves the original objection, or explicitly remain open.

No paid generation was requested. No actual film quality improvement has been
demonstrated. The next revision must preserve the user's comic intent rather
than default to the editor's more sentimental alternate ending.

## Second live revision: CINE-CREATIVE-02

Request `88e01666e5c74aca9995ea92a397a6ba` asked the same Cine session to
replace only segments 09-12, preserve comedy and use 41 seconds. It returned
to idle with 9.5/10.5/10/11-second segments. This tests responsiveness to
explicit editorial feedback, not unguided generalization.

Improved: the paper cup stays consistent; the ending removes smoke, altered
eyes, five-metre printouts and wardrobe changes; warnings are much shorter.

Still not accepted as production-ready:

- The model claims a twenty-metre run versus a two-second swallow proves
  inevitability. It does not explain why a shouted warning cannot stop the
  drink. Spatial distance is not sufficient evidence of auditory delay.
- The new unattended-service policy is in an explanatory preface, not
  clearly communicated through action/dialogue. A warning sticker alone
  does not convey the proposed budget-cut mechanism to viewers.
- Original cold beverage becomes hot sour-plum soup and one barrel becomes
  two without acknowledging continuity/asset changes.
- The old man still tilts his head ninety degrees and performs multiple
  swallowing beats; the scene needlessly retains a difficult physical action.
- Last segment has several spoken lines plus printing, collapse and dropping
  a cup in eleven seconds. No read-through validates its performance budget.

Do not add all these project-specific objections to global instructions.
The transferable lesson is to review what the viewer sees/hears, not the
writer's explanatory claims. Ask the user for comedy-style preference before
treating a restrained or exaggerated candidate as the preferred direction.

## Third live revision: CINE-CREATIVE-03

Request `77371536a4be4bb2a2fbe1b45e5d28ce` required only local replacement
text for segments 09-11. Cine returned to idle with a short patch. It restored
one cold-drink barrel and the black paper cup, withdrew the off-screen policy
explanation, and placed visible swallowing before the first warning.

This is a concrete improvement in event order and constrained revision.
It is not a complete scene acceptance: withdrawing the policy leaves the
unsafe self-service setup unexplained; the cup still covers the face and
swallowing is exaggerated. The response's claim of a complete causal closure
is therefore too strong. Timed dialogue, shot readability and comedy preference
remain untested. No further rewrite should be treated as automatically better
without user preference or a viewing test.

## Current deployment and conclusion

Cine v19 includes the initial story/directing guidance, verified from the
server's actual bundle. The later two short instruction additions remain in
the repository, not in that deployed bundle; trials 02 and 03 received their
specific editorial feedback as messages. Do not attribute all improvement to
skill changes. These three iterations show coachability, not autonomous
mastery, generalization or improved finished-film quality.

## Fourth live trial: cross-scene transfer on v20

Request `5fab76140d874c68bc3d5fd5967b0512` tested a twelve-second bridge
excerpt and a new twenty-second mother/daughter bus-stop scene. The live
session finished. V20's actual bundle had been inspected for the user's
style-follows-script, big-screen-craft instructions before this trial.

The mother/daughter scene uses a concrete exchange of warm food, pauses,
environmental bus sound and a held departure. It follows the requested length,
four-shot ceiling and specified dialogue without added tears or embraces.
However, the daughter holds the admission notice in her right hand in shot
one and uses both hands for the food in shot four without accounting for the
notice. The camera-side and look relationships also remain underspecified.

The bridge revision is not accepted: Cine explicitly rejects supernatural
ease in favour of body weight, bending metal and strenuous shouting. That
changes the comic premise from a composed supernatural employee to a human
struggling rescue worker. Big-screen craft does not require realistic strength.
It also adds steel-grating footholds and changes candy behaviour. Its claim of
unchanged essentials is therefore incomplete. Precise time totals do not prove
the long shouted line can be performed inside its five-second allocation.

Conclusion: some ability to vary tone is visible, but style-sensitive judgement
and object continuity remain unreliable. Do not keep expanding global rules
after every example. Next editorial feedback should ask for preservation of
the intended character contrast and a read-through, not more physics detail.
No generated-film quality or broad autonomous generalization is established.
